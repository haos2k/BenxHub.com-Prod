3:T9dd,<p><strong>Cursor CLI</strong> lets you invoke Cursor's AI capabilities directly from the terminal — no Cursor window required. You can still ask the AI to write code, edit files, or answer questions. For engineers who live in the terminal, it's like having an assistant on call at all times.</p>
<h2 id="why-learn-the-cli">Why Learn the CLI?</h2>
<table>

<th>Using Cursor CLI</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>

<td>Requires SSH + remote setup or sync</td>
<td>Run commands directly in the terminal</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Writing scripts, CI automation</td>
<td>Hard to integrate into a pipeline</td>
<td>Non-interactive mode, one command to run</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Already working in the terminal</td>
<td>Have to switch to the IDE and back</td>
<td>Ask the AI in the same window</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Prefer keyboard-driven workflow</td>
<td>Mouse clicks unavoidable</td>
<td>Everything done with commands</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>In short: the CLI lets the AI follow your workflow, instead of forcing you to adapt to the IDE.</p>
