3:T1363,<p>In practice, systems tend to break not because of "insufficient features" but because of <strong>process and habits</strong>. This checklist can be used as daily guidelines — every pitfall avoided is another time you leave work on time.</p>
<h2 id="recommended-practices">Recommended Practices</h2>
<h3 id="1-backup-must-be-paired-with-restore-drills">1) Backup Must Be Paired with Restore Drills</h3>
<p>Having a backup file doesn't mean you can actually restore from it. <strong>Periodically test a restore</strong> — verify it boots and services are healthy. A backup you've never tested is like a new pair of shoes you've never tried on: you only find out they hurt when you're in battle.</p>
<h3 id="2-snapshot-before-major-changes">2) Snapshot Before Major Changes</h3>
<p>Before updating packages, changing the network, or touching hardware, take a snapshot first. If things go wrong, roll back and skip the reinstall spiral.</p>
<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre style="background-color:#24292e;color:#e1e4e8" tabindex="0" data-language="bash" data-theme="github-dark"><code data-language="bash" data-theme="github-dark" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="color:#B392F0">qm</span><span style="color:#9ECBFF"> snapshot</span><span style="color:#79B8FF"> 100</span><span style="color:#9ECBFF"> before-maintenance</span></span></code></pre></figure>
<h3 id="3-plan-your-update-and-subscription-strategy">3) Plan Your Update and Subscription Strategy</h3>
<p>For production environments: know which environment gets updated first and which gets it later. Understand your subscription and package sources — don't randomly add third-party repos.</p>
<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre style="background-color:#24292e;color:#e1e4e8" tabindex="0" data-language="bash" data-theme="github-dark"><code data-language="bash" data-theme="github-dark" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="color:#6A737D"># Check subscription status</span></span>

<h3 id="4-network-segmentation-and-minimal-exposure">4) Network Segmentation and Minimal Exposure</h3>
<p>Management, storage, and business traffic: <strong>separate when you can</strong> to avoid mutual interference and lateral spread. Save the "one thing breaks everything" scenario for the movies.</p>
<h3 id="5-role-based-access-and-firewall-baseline">5) Role-Based Access and Firewall Baseline</h3>
<p>Don't have everyone sharing a single <code>root</code> account. Create role-based accounts, enable 2FA where possible, and open only the necessary ports in the firewall. Weak password + high privilege = walking naked on the internet.</p>
<h2 id="common-pitfalls">Common Pitfalls</h2>
<h3 id="pitfall-1-layering-hardware-raid-on-top-of-zfsceph">Pitfall 1: Layering Hardware RAID on Top of ZFS/Ceph</h3>
<p>ZFS and Ceph need to <strong>manage disks directly</strong>. Adding a hardware RAID layer on top halves their superpowers — both data protection and observability suffer.</p>
<h3 id="pitfall-2-expecting-ha-from-a-single-node">Pitfall 2: Expecting HA from a Single Node</h3>
<p>HA requires multiple nodes that can take over from each other. With only one machine, don't dream of high availability — that's a "single point of failure waiting to happen."</p>
<h3 id="pitfall-3-deleting-the-base-image-of-a-linked-clone">Pitfall 3: Deleting the Base Image of a Linked Clone</h3>
<p>Linked clones depend on their parent disk. Delete the parent and all the linked VMs underneath break — like pulling out the bottom block in a stack.</p>
<h3 id="pitfall-4-no-capacity-monitoring">Pitfall 4: No Capacity Monitoring</h3>
<p>When <code>local-lvm</code> or backup storage fills up, writes fail and jobs break. <strong>Monitor early, clean up early</strong> — don't wait for an alert at 2 AM before getting up to fight the fire.</p>
<h3 id="pitfall-5-weak-passwords-and-excessive-permissions">Pitfall 5: Weak Passwords and Excessive Permissions</h3>
<p>If the management interface is exposed to the internet, weak passwords and wide-open permissions are an open invitation. Change what needs changing, restrict what needs restricting.</p>
<hr>
<p>Following this checklist won't guarantee zero failures, but it'll save you a lot of unnecessary detours. When in doubt, check the official docs and forums — most pitfalls have been stumbled into by others before you.
Best of luck with your operations — may your VMs run smoothly and your backups always restore! 🦦</p>
