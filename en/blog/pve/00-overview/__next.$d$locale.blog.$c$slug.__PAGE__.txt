3:Tea3,<p>Proxmox VE (Proxmox Virtual Environment) is a Debian-based open-source virtualization platform that integrates <strong>KVM virtual machines</strong> and <strong>LXC containers</strong>, with web management, clustering, backup, and high availability. In short: <strong>VMware's open-source best friend — without the licensing fees.</strong></p>
<p>Compared to VMware ESXi and Hyper-V, PVE's advantages are open-source transparency, low licensing cost (zero, actually), and native support for advanced storage solutions like ZFS and Ceph. It's especially suitable for homelabs and small-to-medium businesses — useful in your home server rack and your company's data center alike. Like a cat that can sleep on the sofa or in a cardboard box — highly adaptable.</p>
<h2 id="use-cases">Use Cases</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Self-hosted NAS, homelab, test environments</strong>: The top choice for home tinkering — if it breaks, just reinstall</li>
<li><strong>SMB virtualization and private cloud</strong>: No competing for cloud quotas with big providers — manage your own machines</li>
<li><strong>Production workloads needing backup, migration, and HA</strong>: For the serious production deployments — PVE can handle it</li>
<li><strong>Not recommended</strong>: Scenarios running a single lightweight container — just use Docker directly for that</li>
</ul>
