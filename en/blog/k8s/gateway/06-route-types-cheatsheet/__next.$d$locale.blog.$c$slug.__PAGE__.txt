b:T416e,<p>If you've read the previous posts, the question that's most likely blocking you now isn't "I don't understand Gateway API" — it's:</p>
<p><strong>"Okay but which one do I actually write — <code>HTTPRoute</code>, <code>GRPCRoute</code>, <code>TCPRoute</code>, or <code>TLSRoute</code>?"</strong></p>
<p>This post exists specifically to untangle that. Keep it as a pocket reference. When a new service comes up, consult this first — it'll save you from writing YAML blindly and getting a lesson from condition statuses.</p>
<h2 id="the-conclusion-first-four-route-types-four-purposes">The Conclusion First: Four Route Types, Four Purposes</h2>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Route Type</th>
<th>Traffic Layer</th>
<th>What It Inspects</th>
<th>Best For</th>
<th>Not For</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td><code>HTTPRoute</code></td>
<td>L7 HTTP/HTTPS</td>
<td>host, path, header, query, method</td>
<td>Websites, REST APIs, general web traffic</td>
<td>Raw TCP, TLS passthrough</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>GRPCRoute</code></td>
<td>L7 gRPC</td>
<td>hostname, service, method, header</td>
<td>gRPC service/method routing</td>
<td>General REST APIs, raw TCP</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>TCPRoute</code></td>
<td>L4 TCP</td>
<td>Listener and connection-level info</td>
<td>Databases, custom TCP protocols, non-HTTP services</td>
<td>Scenarios needing path/header inspection</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>TLSRoute</code></td>
<td>L4/L5 TLS</td>
<td>TLS SNI hostname</td>
<td>TLS passthrough, preserving end-to-end encryption</td>
<td>Needing to inspect HTTP details after decryption</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>If you want an ultra-short version:</p>
<ul>
<li>Inspect <code>path</code> or <code>header</code> → <code>HTTPRoute</code></li>
<li>Inspect gRPC <code>service/method</code> → <code>GRPCRoute</code></li>
<li>Just forwarding TCP connections → <code>TCPRoute</code></li>
<li>No TLS termination, SNI routing only → <code>TLSRoute</code></li>
</ul>
<h2 id="three-step-decision-stop-guessing">Three-Step Decision: Stop Guessing</h2>
<p>Use this order:</p>
<ol>
<li>Will the Gateway parse HTTP semantics from this traffic?</li>
<li>If yes, is it regular HTTP/REST, or gRPC?</li>
<li>If no, is it raw TCP, or TLS passthrough?</li>
</ol>
<p>Mapped out:</p>
<ul>
<li>Has HTTP path/header/method to inspect → <code>HTTPRoute</code></li>
<li>Has gRPC service/method to inspect → <code>GRPCRoute</code></li>
<li>No L7 rules, just TCP forwarding → <code>TCPRoute</code></li>
<li>Want TLS preserved to the backend → <code>TLSRoute</code></li>
</ul>
<p>This decision matters a lot. Many people instinctively write <code>HTTPRoute</code> first, then realize halfway through that they're dealing with database traffic. The entire YAML enters "what am I even doing" mode.</p>

<h2 id="four-common-pitfalls">Four Common Pitfalls</h2>
<h3 id="1-grpc-doesnt-necessarily-require-grpcroute">1. gRPC Doesn't Necessarily Require <code>GRPCRoute</code></h3>
<p>If you just need gRPC traffic to flow, some implementations may support it via <code>HTTPRoute</code> since gRPC runs over HTTP/2.
But if you want to match on gRPC service/method semantics, <code>GRPCRoute</code> is the more correct and readable choice.</p>
<h3 id="2-tlsroute-is-not-https-but-advanced">2. <code>TLSRoute</code> Is Not "HTTPS but Advanced"</h3>
<p>This is the most common misconception.</p>
<ul>
<li><code>HTTPS listener + HTTPRoute</code>: Gateway terminates TLS, then applies HTTP rules</li>
<li><code>TLS listener + TLSRoute</code>: Gateway does NOT terminate TLS, routes based on SNI only</li>
</ul>
<p>One decrypts and plays at L7. The other keeps traffic encrypted and does passthrough. Completely different worlds.</p>
<h3 id="3-tcproute-cannot-see-pathheader">3. <code>TCPRoute</code> Cannot See Path/Header</h3>
<p>If your requirement is:</p>
<ul>
<li><code>/api</code> goes to A</li>
<li><code>/admin</code> goes to B</li>
</ul>
<p>That's not <code>TCPRoute</code>'s job.
<code>TCPRoute</code> doesn't understand HTTP details. Forcing those requirements into it is just setting yourself up for frustration.</p>
<h3 id="4-parentrefssectionname-is-important">4. <code>parentRefs.sectionName</code> Is Important</h3>
<p>When a Gateway has multiple listeners, use <code>sectionName</code>:</p>
<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre style="background-color:#0d1117;color:#e6edf3" tabindex="0" data-language="yaml" data-theme="github-dark-default"><code data-language="yaml" data-theme="github-dark-default" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">parentRefs</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">:</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#E6EDF3">  - </span><span style="color:#7EE787">name</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#A5D6FF">eg</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">    sectionName</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#A5D6FF">https</span></span></code></pre></figure>
<p>This ensures your route attaches precisely to the listener you intend.
Without it, you might think you're booking a VIP room and end up in the wrong hall entirely.</p>
<h2 id="one-line-summary">One-Line Summary</h2>
<p>First identify what you're working with: <strong>HTTP, gRPC, TCP, or TLS passthrough</strong> — then pick the right route type.
Get the route right and 80% of your YAML flows naturally. Get it wrong and you're trapped in a battle with condition statuses.</p>

d:T416e,<p>If you've read the previous posts, the question that's most likely blocking you now isn't "I don't understand Gateway API" — it's:</p>
<p><strong>"Okay but which one do I actually write — <code>HTTPRoute</code>, <code>GRPCRoute</code>, <code>TCPRoute</code>, or <code>TLSRoute</code>?"</strong></p>
<p>This post exists specifically to untangle that. Keep it as a pocket reference. When a new service comes up, consult this first — it'll save you from writing YAML blindly and getting a lesson from condition statuses.</p>
<h2 id="the-conclusion-first-four-route-types-four-purposes">The Conclusion First: Four Route Types, Four Purposes</h2>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Route Type</th>
<th>Traffic Layer</th>
<th>What It Inspects</th>
<th>Best For</th>
<th>Not For</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td><code>HTTPRoute</code></td>
<td>L7 HTTP/HTTPS</td>
<td>host, path, header, query, method</td>
<td>Websites, REST APIs, general web traffic</td>
<td>Raw TCP, TLS passthrough</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>GRPCRoute</code></td>
<td>L7 gRPC</td>
<td>hostname, service, method, header</td>
<td>gRPC service/method routing</td>
<td>General REST APIs, raw TCP</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>TCPRoute</code></td>
<td>L4 TCP</td>
<td>Listener and connection-level info</td>
<td>Databases, custom TCP protocols, non-HTTP services</td>
<td>Scenarios needing path/header inspection</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>TLSRoute</code></td>
<td>L4/L5 TLS</td>
<td>TLS SNI hostname</td>
<td>TLS passthrough, preserving end-to-end encryption</td>
<td>Needing to inspect HTTP details after decryption</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>If you want an ultra-short version:</p>
<ul>
<li>Inspect <code>path</code> or <code>header</code> → <code>HTTPRoute</code></li>
<li>Inspect gRPC <code>service/method</code> → <code>GRPCRoute</code></li>
<li>Just forwarding TCP connections → <code>TCPRoute</code></li>
<li>No TLS termination, SNI routing only → <code>TLSRoute</code></li>
</ul>
<h2 id="three-step-decision-stop-guessing">Three-Step Decision: Stop Guessing</h2>
<p>Use this order:</p>
<ol>
<li>Will the Gateway parse HTTP semantics from this traffic?</li>
<li>If yes, is it regular HTTP/REST, or gRPC?</li>
<li>If no, is it raw TCP, or TLS passthrough?</li>
</ol>
<p>Mapped out:</p>
<ul>
<li>Has HTTP path/header/method to inspect → <code>HTTPRoute</code></li>
<li>Has gRPC service/method to inspect → <code>GRPCRoute</code></li>
<li>No L7 rules, just TCP forwarding → <code>TCPRoute</code></li>
<li>Want TLS preserved to the backend → <code>TLSRoute</code></li>
</ul>
<p>This decision matters a lot. Many people instinctively write <code>HTTPRoute</code> first, then realize halfway through that they're dealing with database traffic. The entire YAML enters "what am I even doing" mode.</p>

<h2 id="four-common-pitfalls">Four Common Pitfalls</h2>
<h3 id="1-grpc-doesnt-necessarily-require-grpcroute">1. gRPC Doesn't Necessarily Require <code>GRPCRoute</code></h3>
<p>If you just need gRPC traffic to flow, some implementations may support it via <code>HTTPRoute</code> since gRPC runs over HTTP/2.
But if you want to match on gRPC service/method semantics, <code>GRPCRoute</code> is the more correct and readable choice.</p>
<h3 id="2-tlsroute-is-not-https-but-advanced">2. <code>TLSRoute</code> Is Not "HTTPS but Advanced"</h3>
<p>This is the most common misconception.</p>
<ul>
<li><code>HTTPS listener + HTTPRoute</code>: Gateway terminates TLS, then applies HTTP rules</li>
<li><code>TLS listener + TLSRoute</code>: Gateway does NOT terminate TLS, routes based on SNI only</li>
</ul>
<p>One decrypts and plays at L7. The other keeps traffic encrypted and does passthrough. Completely different worlds.</p>
<h3 id="3-tcproute-cannot-see-pathheader">3. <code>TCPRoute</code> Cannot See Path/Header</h3>
<p>If your requirement is:</p>
<ul>
<li><code>/api</code> goes to A</li>
<li><code>/admin</code> goes to B</li>
</ul>
<p>That's not <code>TCPRoute</code>'s job.
<code>TCPRoute</code> doesn't understand HTTP details. Forcing those requirements into it is just setting yourself up for frustration.</p>
<h3 id="4-parentrefssectionname-is-important">4. <code>parentRefs.sectionName</code> Is Important</h3>
<p>When a Gateway has multiple listeners, use <code>sectionName</code>:</p>
<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre style="background-color:#0d1117;color:#e6edf3" tabindex="0" data-language="yaml" data-theme="github-dark-default"><code data-language="yaml" data-theme="github-dark-default" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">parentRefs</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">:</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#E6EDF3">  - </span><span style="color:#7EE787">name</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#A5D6FF">eg</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">    sectionName</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#A5D6FF">https</span></span></code></pre></figure>
<p>This ensures your route attaches precisely to the listener you intend.
Without it, you might think you're booking a VIP room and end up in the wrong hall entirely.</p>
<h2 id="one-line-summary">One-Line Summary</h2>
<p>First identify what you're working with: <strong>HTTP, gRPC, TCP, or TLS passthrough</strong> — then pick the right route type.
Get the route right and 80% of your YAML flows naturally. Get it wrong and you're trapped in a battle with condition statuses.</p>
