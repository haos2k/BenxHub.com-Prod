1c:T25c9,<p>"It runs" and "the system is actually stable" are two completely different universes. Most ingress layer incidents aren't caused by Envoy Gateway being insufficient — they're caused by config that was set up too casually, turning operations into an ongoing surprise box. This post compiles the most practical recommendations into a checklist.</p>
<h2 id="recommended-practices">Recommended Practices</h2>
<h3 id="1-be-clear-about-who-owns-gateway-vs-who-owns-routes">1. Be Clear About Who Owns Gateway vs. Who Owns Routes</h3>
<p>If multiple people are involved, split the responsibilities:</p>
<ul>
<li>Platform team manages <code>GatewayClass</code>, <code>Gateway</code></li>
<li>App teams manage their own <code>HTTPRoute</code></li>
</ul>
<p>The benefit: the ingress layer's security and exposure surface doesn't get randomly modified by every app that comes along, while app teams still retain routing flexibility. This is exactly why Gateway API's design is worth using — don't flatten it back into a monolithic YAML blob.</p>
<h3 id="2-treat-tls-as-a-default-from-the-start">2. Treat TLS as a Default from the Start</h3>
<p>Don't think "HTTP first, add HTTPS later."
That "later" almost always turns into a Friday night emergency.</p>
<p>At minimum:</p>
<ul>
<li>External listeners default to HTTPS</li>
<li>Certificate sources have a defined management process</li>
<li>Test, self-signed, and production CA workflows are kept separate</li>
</ul>
<p>If you already have cert-manager, integrate it. Automated certificate rotation is far more reliable than manually patching Secrets every renewal cycle.</p>
<h3 id="3-write-route-rules-explicitly--dont-over-optimize-for-convenience">3. Write Route Rules Explicitly — Don't Over-Optimize for Convenience</h3>
<p>Rules like this are convenient but tend to become black holes:</p>
<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre style="background-color:#0d1117;color:#e6edf3" tabindex="0" data-language="yaml" data-theme="github-dark-default"><code data-language="yaml" data-theme="github-dark-default" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">rules</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">:</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#E6EDF3">  - </span><span style="color:#7EE787">backendRefs</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">:</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#E6EDF3">      - </span><span style="color:#7EE787">name</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#A5D6FF">backend</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">        port</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#79C0FF">8080</span></span></code></pre></figure>
<p>It's not wrong — but as the system grows, too many "catch everything" rules make debugging painful.
If you can write <code>hostnames</code>, if you can write <code>path</code>, write them clearly.</p>
<h3 id="4-use-small-traffic-cuts--dont-go-all-in-at-once">4. Use Small Traffic Cuts — Don't Go All-In at Once</h3>
<p>When launching a new version, cut a small percentage of traffic first:</p>
<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre style="background-color:#0d1117;color:#e6edf3" tabindex="0" data-language="yaml" data-theme="github-dark-default"><code data-language="yaml" data-theme="github-dark-default" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">backendRefs</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">:</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#E6EDF3">  - </span><span style="color:#7EE787">name</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#A5D6FF">api-v1</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">    port</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#79C0FF">8080</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">    weight</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#79C0FF">90</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#E6EDF3">  - </span><span style="color:#7EE787">name</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#A5D6FF">api-v2</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">    port</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#79C0FF">8080</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">    weight</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#79C0FF">10</span></span></code></pre></figure>
<p>This is much gentler than "flip the entire service selector in one shot."
When things go wrong, rolling back is also much faster — at 3 AM, you'll be very grateful for this.</p>
<h3 id="5-build-the-habit-of-checking-status-and-events">5. Build the Habit of Checking <code>status</code> and Events</h3>
<p>Just reading YAML isn't enough — you need to see if the controller actually accepted your config:</p>
<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre style="background-color:#0d1117;color:#e6edf3" tabindex="0" data-language="bash" data-theme="github-dark-default"><code data-language="bash" data-theme="github-dark-default" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="color:#FFA657">kubectl</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> get</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> gateway</span><span style="color:#79C0FF"> -A</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#FFA657">kubectl</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> get</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> httproute</span><span style="color:#79C0FF"> -A</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#FFA657">kubectl</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> describe</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> gateway</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> eg</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#FFA657">kubectl</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> describe</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> httproute</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> app-route</span></span></code></pre></figure>
<p>These categories of problems often can't be spotted just from <code>spec</code>:</p>
<ul>
<li>Route not accepted</li>
<li>Cross-namespace reference blocked</li>
<li>Listener status anomalies</li>
</ul>
<p>The worst thing isn't a bug — it's "I thought it took effect, but it was never even picked up."</p>
<h2 id="common-pitfalls">Common Pitfalls</h2>
<h3 id="pitfall-1-treating-envoy-gateway-as-a-dumb-forwarding-black-box">Pitfall 1: Treating Envoy Gateway as a Dumb Forwarding Black Box</h3>
<p>It's not just moving packets from A to B.
The official docs explicitly position it as a Kubernetes-native API Gateway with room to layer on more traffic and security policy. If you only use it as an Ingress replacement, you're massively underutilizing it.</p>
<h3 id="pitfall-2-referencing-cross-namespace-certificates-without-referencegrant">Pitfall 2: Referencing Cross-Namespace Certificates Without <code>ReferenceGrant</code></h3>
<p>Many people see <code>certificateRefs</code> supports a namespace field and just point across namespaces directly, then wonder why nothing happens.
The answer usually isn't haunting — it's a missing <code>ReferenceGrant</code>.</p>
<h3 id="pitfall-3-only-testing-the-happy-path">Pitfall 3: Only Testing the Happy Path</h3>
<p>Don't just test:</p>
<ul>
<li><code>curl http://...</code> succeeds once</li>
</ul>
<p>Also test:</p>
<ul>
<li>Different hostnames</li>
<li>Different paths</li>
<li>TLS certificate validity</li>
<li>Whether traffic split ratios behave as expected</li>
</ul>
<p>The ingress layer is most dangerous when happy paths look perfect but edge cases break immediately.</p>
<h3 id="pitfall-4-using-demo-config-directly-in-production">Pitfall 4: Using Demo Config Directly in Production</h3>
<p>The official quickstart is excellent for learning, but it's positioned as a quickstart.
For production, at minimum add:</p>
<ul>
<li>Certificate automation</li>
<li>Monitoring and alerting</li>
<li>Change management process</li>
<li>Route naming conventions</li>
</ul>
<p>Otherwise, today's quickstart becomes tomorrow's quick-fall.</p>
<h2 id="a-minimal-pre-launch-checklist">A Minimal Pre-Launch Checklist</h2>
<p>Use this before going live:</p>
<ul>
<li>Does the <code>Gateway</code> listener clearly separate HTTP / HTTPS?</li>
<li>Is the certificate Secret and rotation process well-defined?</li>
<li>Does <code>HTTPRoute</code> have clear <code>hostnames</code> / <code>matches</code>?</li>
<li>Are significant changes deployed using small traffic cuts?</li>
<li>Can you quickly locate problems from <code>status</code> and events?</li>
</ul>
<p>If all five are in place, you're already operating at a higher level than "it works so it's fine."</p>
<h2 id="one-line-summary">One-Line Summary</h2>
<p>The real value of Envoy Gateway isn't just routing traffic — it's making your ingress layer configuration more standardized, divisible, and governable. Features are temporary; operations are long-term. Being kind to your future self actually matters.</p>

<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre style="background-color:#0d1117;color:#e6edf3" tabindex="0" data-language="yaml" data-theme="github-dark-default"><code data-language="yaml" data-theme="github-dark-default" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">rules</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">:</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#E6EDF3">  - </span><span style="color:#7EE787">backendRefs</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">:</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#E6EDF3">      - </span><span style="color:#7EE787">name</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#A5D6FF">backend</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">        port</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#79C0FF">8080</span></span></code></pre></figure>
<p>It's not wrong — but as the system grows, too many "catch everything" rules make debugging painful.
If you can write <code>hostnames</code>, if you can write <code>path</code>, write them clearly.</p>
<h3 id="4-use-small-traffic-cuts--dont-go-all-in-at-once">4. Use Small Traffic Cuts — Don't Go All-In at Once</h3>
<p>When launching a new version, cut a small percentage of traffic first:</p>
<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre style="background-color:#0d1117;color:#e6edf3" tabindex="0" data-language="yaml" data-theme="github-dark-default"><code data-language="yaml" data-theme="github-dark-default" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">backendRefs</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">:</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#E6EDF3">  - </span><span style="color:#7EE787">name</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#A5D6FF">api-v1</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">    port</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#79C0FF">8080</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">    weight</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#79C0FF">90</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#E6EDF3">  - </span><span style="color:#7EE787">name</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#A5D6FF">api-v2</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">    port</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#79C0FF">8080</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#7EE787">    weight</span><span style="color:#E6EDF3">: </span><span style="color:#79C0FF">10</span></span></code></pre></figure>
<p>This is much gentler than "flip the entire service selector in one shot."
When things go wrong, rolling back is also much faster — at 3 AM, you'll be very grateful for this.</p>
<h3 id="5-build-the-habit-of-checking-status-and-events">5. Build the Habit of Checking <code>status</code> and Events</h3>
<p>Just reading YAML isn't enough — you need to see if the controller actually accepted your config:</p>
<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre style="background-color:#0d1117;color:#e6edf3" tabindex="0" data-language="bash" data-theme="github-dark-default"><code data-language="bash" data-theme="github-dark-default" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="color:#FFA657">kubectl</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> get</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> gateway</span><span style="color:#79C0FF"> -A</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#FFA657">kubectl</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> get</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> httproute</span><span style="color:#79C0FF"> -A</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#FFA657">kubectl</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> describe</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> gateway</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> eg</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="color:#FFA657">kubectl</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> describe</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> httproute</span><span style="color:#A5D6FF"> app-route</span></span></code></pre></figure>
<p>These categories of problems often can't be spotted just from <code>spec</code>:</p>
<ul>
<li>Route not accepted</li>
<li>Cross-namespace reference blocked</li>
<li>Listener status anomalies</li>
</ul>
<p>The worst thing isn't a bug — it's "I thought it took effect, but it was never even picked up."</p>
<h2 id="common-pitfalls">Common Pitfalls</h2>
<h3 id="pitfall-1-treating-envoy-gateway-as-a-dumb-forwarding-black-box">Pitfall 1: Treating Envoy Gateway as a Dumb Forwarding Black Box</h3>
<p>It's not just moving packets from A to B.
The official docs explicitly position it as a Kubernetes-native API Gateway with room to layer on more traffic and security policy. If you only use it as an Ingress replacement, you're massively underutilizing it.</p>
<h3 id="pitfall-2-referencing-cross-namespace-certificates-without-referencegrant">Pitfall 2: Referencing Cross-Namespace Certificates Without <code>ReferenceGrant</code></h3>
<p>Many people see <code>certificateRefs</code> supports a namespace field and just point across namespaces directly, then wonder why nothing happens.
The answer usually isn't haunting — it's a missing <code>ReferenceGrant</code>.</p>
<h3 id="pitfall-3-only-testing-the-happy-path">Pitfall 3: Only Testing the Happy Path</h3>
<p>Don't just test:</p>
<ul>
<li><code>curl http://...</code> succeeds once</li>
</ul>
<p>Also test:</p>
<ul>
<li>Different hostnames</li>
<li>Different paths</li>
<li>TLS certificate validity</li>
<li>Whether traffic split ratios behave as expected</li>
</ul>
<p>The ingress layer is most dangerous when happy paths look perfect but edge cases break immediately.</p>
<h3 id="pitfall-4-using-demo-config-directly-in-production">Pitfall 4: Using Demo Config Directly in Production</h3>
<p>The official quickstart is excellent for learning, but it's positioned as a quickstart.
For production, at minimum add:</p>
<ul>
<li>Certificate automation</li>
<li>Monitoring and alerting</li>
<li>Change management process</li>
<li>Route naming conventions</li>
</ul>
<p>Otherwise, today's quickstart becomes tomorrow's quick-fall.</p>
<h2 id="a-minimal-pre-launch-checklist">A Minimal Pre-Launch Checklist</h2>
<p>Use this before going live:</p>
<ul>
<li>Does the <code>Gateway</code> listener clearly separate HTTP / HTTPS?</li>
<li>Is the certificate Secret and rotation process well-defined?</li>
<li>Does <code>HTTPRoute</code> have clear <code>hostnames</code> / <code>matches</code>?</li>
<li>Are significant changes deployed using small traffic cuts?</li>
<li>Can you quickly locate problems from <code>status</code> and events?</li>
</ul>
<p>If all five are in place, you're already operating at a higher level than "it works so it's fine."</p>
<h2 id="one-line-summary">One-Line Summary</h2>
<p>The real value of Envoy Gateway isn't just routing traffic — it's making your ingress layer configuration more standardized, divisible, and governable. Features are temporary; operations are long-term. Being kind to your future self actually matters.</p>
